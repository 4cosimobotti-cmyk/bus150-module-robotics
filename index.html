My plan in one line: Build cheap in Mexico, keep the parts North American so we don't get hit with tariffs, and keep a warehouse in Florida so customers get their stuff fast. It's like buying a stock: I'd rather start small with a stop-loss than go all in on day one.

1Where should Module Robotics build its modules?Mexico · South Florida · both Mexico. We still design in Florida. Labor is way cheaper. About $9 to build one module in Mexico vs. $48 in the US. Trucks still get to US customers in a few days. Every box crosses a border, so tariffs and politics can hurt us. We also lose the "Made in USA" label. If the US drops the tariff break for USMCA goods, or the yearly USMCA review looks like the deal is ending.
2How does a five-person company legally start producing?Shelter provider · own Mexican company · contract manufacturer · own Florida shop + MEP Shelter provider (like Tetakawi). We can start in about 30 days instead of 8 to 12 months. They handle the legal stuff, payroll, and customs. We just build. We pay them a monthly fee, and less of the business is fully ours. Once we're big enough that the fee costs more than running our own company in Mexico, we set up our own.
3What crosses the border, and in what form?Global parts · North American parts · finished robots · modules Modules made with North American parts. Keeps our LEGO idea. North American parts mean we qualify for USMCA, so no 10% tariff and no 50% Mexican tax on Chinese parts. Parts cost more and there are fewer suppliers. More paperwork. If too much of our parts start coming from outside North America. We stay honest so we don't end up like Ford paying $365 million.
4Who, or what, builds the modules?People in Mexico · automated cell in Florida · rented robots · mix People in Mexico for now. Rent robots later. No huge robot bill while we're small. People can adjust if the design changes. Mexico raised wages 13% and is cutting the workweek to 40 hours with the same pay, so our cost edge shrinks over time. If we pass about 10,000 modules a year, or Mexican labor gets close to $9 an hour, we look at automation.

5How do the modules reach the customer?Rail · truck · short haul from Florida · regional warehouse Truck to a warehouse in South Florida. Modules are small, so trucks work fine. With stock in the warehouse, a team that orders Monday still gets parts before a Saturday competition. Money sits on shelves as inventory. Border lines can add days. When we're shipping a full container every week, we switch to train (CPKC) because it's cheaper.
6What exactly do we sell, and to whom first?Global distributors · direct to US customers · modules-as-a-service Sell direct to US customers. Tell Germany "not yet." We keep all the profit and talk to customers ourselves. Rethink Robotics bet on one foreign customer and went under. I don't want that. We grow slower and miss early sales in Europe. Once we have about 500 repeat US customers, we sign one or two foreign distributors.
7How hard do we commit, and how would we back out?Commit fully · stage it · run both · wait and watch Stage it. Rent, lease, and sign short contracts. Easy to back out. Tesla paused its Mexico plant before building it, so it lost land, not a whole factory. Costs a little more per module and we have less control. Same as stop 1. If the tariff break ends, we move assembly to a small Florida shop.
8Who do we serve first?School teams · makers · university labs · small manufacturers School robotics teams. They have a set season, they come back every year, and they talk to each other. goBILDA already proved this works. Small budgets, and sales only come in during the season. If school sales are flat two seasons in a row, we start selling to small factories.

What does that cost us?
If the card hits tariffs or USMCA, I'd hedge. Keep Mexico running and open a small shop in Florida too. Since we only rented and leased, it costs us a second lease and a few new hires, not a whole factory. If the card doesn't hit our switch signal, we stay.

Looking back

Did we switch paths at any stop? Which one, and why?
Not fully. I stayed with Mexico, but I put the warehouse in Florida because school teams care about speed more than anything.
After choosing our first customer (stop 8), which earlier choice would we change?
Stop 4. School orders spike right before the season, so I'd hire extra workers in the fall and stock up early. It's like training for a meet: you peak for the season, not all year. Cheap labor only helps if the modules are on the shelf before the rush.
